Receiving a formal Show Cause Notice (SCN) in **Form GST DRC-01** under **Section 73** or **Section 74** of the CGST Act is the most serious departmental action most businesses will face. If mishandled or ignored, these notices convert into non-appealable recovery orders under Section 79, empowering tax authorities to freeze your company's bank accounts.

However, a notice is merely an allegation, not an adjudicated liability. Under Indian tax jurisprudence, the burden of proof rests heavily on the tax department.

In this guide, we analyze the critical statutory differences between Section 73 and Section 74, statutory limitation deadlines, penalty mitigation strategies, and how to build an airtight legal defense.

## The Critical Difference: Section 73 vs Section 74

| Parameter | Section 73 (Non-Fraud / Bona Fide) | Section 74 (Fraud / Wilful Misstatement) |
|---|---|---|
| **Charge / Intent** | Tax unpaid/short paid or ITC wrongly availed **without** intention to evade tax | Tax unpaid/short paid or ITC wrongly availed by reason of **fraud, wilful misstatement, or suppression of facts** |
| **Limitation for Final Order** | **3 years** from due date of Annual Return (GSTR-9) | **5 years** from due date of Annual Return (GSTR-9) |
| **Limitation for Issuing SCN** | At least **3 months prior** to the 3-year order deadline | At least **6 months prior** to the 5-year order deadline |
| **Payment Before SCN** | 0% Penalty (Pay tax + Sec 50 interest only) | **15% Penalty** of tax amount |
| **Payment within 30 days of SCN** | 0% Penalty (Pay tax + Sec 50 interest only) | **25% Penalty** of tax amount |
| **Payment within 30 days of Order** | 10% of tax or ₹10,000 (whichever is higher) | **50% Penalty** of tax amount |
| **Adjudicated Order Penalty** | 10% of tax or ₹10,000 | **100% Penalty** of tax amount |

## 4 Solid Legal Defenses to Invalidate an SCN

When formulating your defense in Form DRC-06, evaluate these four jurisdictional grounds:

### 1. The Limitation Defense (Time-Barred Notice)
Check the exact financial year of the notice. Under Section 73(10), the department must issue the final order within 3 years from the due date of GSTR-9 for that financial year. If the SCN was issued less than 3 months before the statutory cutoff, the notice is **void ab initio** (illegal from the inception).

### 2. Failure to Establish "Mens Rea" (Wrongful Invocation of Section 74)
Tax officers routinely invoke Section 74 simply to take advantage of the extended 5-year limitation period when the normal 3-year period under Section 73 has expired.  
*Legal Counter:* Under landmark Supreme Court rulings (such as *Uniworth Textiles Ltd.* and *Continental Foundation*), "suppression of facts" cannot be equated with mere inadvertence or clerical omission. The department must produce positive documentary evidence demonstrating deliberate intent to evade tax. If they cannot, the entire notice collapses for lack of jurisdiction.

### 3. Violation of Natural Justice (Section 75(4))
Under **Section 75(4) of the CGST Act**:
> *"An opportunity of hearing shall be granted where a request is received in writing from the person chargeable with tax or penalty, or where any adverse decision is contemplated against such person."*

Always explicitly tick the "Request Personal Hearing" box and state:  
*"The taxpayer specifically prays for a personal hearing before any adverse decision or order is contemplated."*  
If the officer issues an order without scheduling an opportunity for personal hearing, High Courts will routinely set aside the demand on grounds of violation of natural justice.

### 4. Vague and Unsubstantiated Allegations
An SCN that merely attaches an automated spreadsheet without explaining the factual foundation or specific legal provisions breached is legally defective. Taxpayers have a constitutional right to know the precise case they have to answer.

## Step-by-Step Response Protocol (Form DRC-06)

1. **Download Notice & Summary:** Log in to `gst.gov.in` → Services → User Services → View Notices and Orders. Note the Reference Number (DRC-01) and Date.
2. **Reconcile Financial Records:** Match the alleged discrepancies with your audited books, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B, and bank statements.
3. **Draft the Written Submission:**  
   - Preliminary Objections (Limitation, Jurisdiction, Non-applicability of Section 74).
   - Factual Rebuttal on Merits (Itemized breakdown).
   - Legal Precedents & CBIC Circulars.
   - Formal Prayer for dropping proceedings and granting personal hearing.
4. **Upload DRC-06 Online:** Submit the reply along with documentary exhibits (ledger extracts, bank statements, supplier confirmations) under 5MB PDF attachments.

## Mitigating Penalties via Voluntary Settlement

If upon investigation you discover that a genuine liability was indeed missed:
- Pay the tax liability + interest under Section 50 using **Form DRC-03**.
- If done within **30 days** of receiving the Section 73 notice, **all penalties are completely waived**, and proceedings are legally deemed concluded under Section 73(8).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### What if I need more time to gather records?
You can file an interim letter on the portal under Form DRC-06 requesting an extension of time (typically 15 to 30 days) on reasonable grounds such as audit closure, medical reasons, or volume of records required.

### Can an officer attach my bank account during SCN proceedings?
Under Section 83, provisional attachment of bank accounts is permissible only during the pendency of proceedings under Sections 62, 63, 64, 67, 73, or 74 to protect government revenue in cases of serious fraud. The CBIC has issued strict guidelines that provisional attachment must not be used as a routine recovery tool against regular, operating businesses.
