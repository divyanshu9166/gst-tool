The GST Council's September 2025 rate rationalisation is often called **"GST 2.0"** in public discussion. It changed many product and service entries, with revised rates generally effective from **22 September 2025**. The published schedules include a 5% merit rate, an 18% standard rate and a 40% special rate, alongside nil or exempt entries. The change did not make one rate apply to every item in a broad product category: classification and the current notification entry still control.

> **Editorial note:** "GST 2.0" is a shorthand, not a separate tax law. Apply the rate in the current Central Tax (Rate), State/UT tax-rate or Integrated Tax (Rate) notification for the item's classification and the transaction's time of supply. The 2025 changes were not a July 2026 blanket rate reset.

If you run a small business, a retail outlet, or a manufacturing firm, these changes directly impact your working capital, your MRPs, and your compliance overhead. In this comprehensive guide, we will break down exactly what GST 2.0 means, which products moved slabs, and how to adapt your billing. Before updating enterprise ERPs, always cross-reference specific goods using the [GST Rate Finder](/gst-rate-finder) or verify classification rules in our [HSN Codes](/hsn-codes) library.

## 1. What is GST 2.0? The New Rate Structure

The 2025 exercise rationalised many rates. These headline bands are a useful orientation, but they are not a substitute for checking the exact HSN/SAC entry and any conditions:

- **Nil or exempt entries:** Apply only to the goods or services specifically listed and subject to the entry's conditions.
- **5%:** Many essential and merit goods moved to this rate; it also applies to some services and product categories under their own entries.
- **18%:** The standard rate for many goods and services, including entries shifted from other rates.
- **40% special rate:** Applies to specifically listed luxury and demerit goods. Some tobacco and pan-masala rate changes were implemented later, from 1 February 2026.

### Why the Change?
The purpose was to rationalise selected schedules and simplify parts of the rate structure. Classification still matters: a product description alone may not identify the correct tariff entry or rate.

## 2. Complete List of Items that Moved Slabs

Here is a detailed breakdown of the major industries and products affected by the GST 2.0 rate changes.

### Verified examples from the official 2025 rate FAQs

The Ministry of Finance's GST rate FAQ records several specific changes. Examples include agricultural machinery and medical devices moving to 5%, bicycles and parts moving from 12% to 5%, three-wheelers and goods transport vehicles moving from 28% to 18%, and qualifying large cars moving to 40%. The descriptions and HSN limits in the official notification control each case; these examples do not set rates for similar-looking products.

### Petrol, cigarettes and flats: check the separate rules

- **Petrol and diesel:** Petrol (motor spirit) and high-speed diesel are not subject to GST until a commencement date is notified under Section 9(2) of the CGST Act. A general GST 2.0 slab should not be applied to fuel at the pump.
- **Cigarettes and specified tobacco products:** The September 2025 GST Council FAQ said their revised rates would take effect later. Subsequent notifications set the revised treatment for specified tobacco and pan-masala products from **1 February 2026**: specified biris were added at 9% central tax (generally 18% combined GST), while listed cigarettes and other products were added at 20% central tax (generally 40% combined GST); specified compensation cess was set to nil. Check the exact product and tariff entry; do not label this a July 2026 change.
- **Flats:** There is no single GST rate for every flat. Qualifying construction of residential apartments by promoters is generally subject to the concessional 1% rate for affordable apartments or 5% for other residential apartments, without ITC and subject to the notification's definitions and conditions. A flat's completion and sale facts also matter.

The report's proposed "July 2026" framing is not supported by the official sources checked. This existing guide covers the September 2025 rationalisation and later notified tobacco changes. ## 3. How These Changes Impact Small Businesses

While the government aimed for simplicity, the transition period requires careful management by MSMEs. Here are the three biggest impacts on your daily operations:

### MRP Revisions and Relabeling
If a listed item changed rate, calculate the effect on taxable value, tax and margin using its exact notification entry. Any revision to a packaged product's declared MRP must also follow applicable Legal Metrology requirements; do not assume a generic relabelling permission applies to every product or date.

To calculate exactly what your new MRP should be while protecting your base margin, use our free [MRP Revision Calculator](/mrp-revision-calculator/).

### Input Tax Credit (ITC) Reversals
When an output rate changes, do not automatically reverse all existing ITC or assume a refund is available. The 56th Council FAQ explains that duly availed credit remains subject to the CGST Act, while supplies that become exempt can require proportionate reversal. Inverted-duty refunds have separate statutory conditions.

Confused if you need to reverse ITC? Try our [ITC Reversal Checker](/itc-reversal-checker) to get a quick Yes/No answer based on your scenario.

### Invoice Management System (IMS) Readiness
The GST portal's Invoice Management System (IMS) lets recipients review supplier-reported records using the actions available for each document. GSTN guidance has described IMS as an optional facility with deemed acceptance for eligible records when no action is taken. Review your records and current portal guidance; see our [IMS guide](/blog/gst-invoice-management-system-ims-guide) for the workflow and official references.

## 4. Checklist: Are You GST 2.0 Ready?

If you haven't fully adapted to the September 2025 changes, follow this quick compliance checklist:

1. **Update ERP/Billing Software:** Map each product or service to the current notification entry and effective date. Do not remove a rate option from a billing system solely because of a headline summary.
2. **Review HSN Master:** Do a bulk review of your product master data. Verify the HSN codes against the new CBIC rate notifications. (You can use our [HSN Code Lookup](/hsn-code-lookup) tool for this).
3. **Re-calculate Margins:** Recalculate prices only for the product entries whose rate changed, taking input tax credit eligibility and contract terms into account.
4. **Inform Customers:** Explain a rate or invoice change accurately. A buyer's ITC eligibility depends on statutory conditions and is not guaranteed merely because a higher tax rate was charged.
5. **Check Freight Rates:** Transport logistics (GTA) remain tricky. Make sure you are calculating Reverse Charge correctly using our [GST on Freight Calculator](/gst-on-freight-calculator).

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions about GST 2.0 Rates

**Q: Can an invoice still show 12% GST?** The correct rate depends on the exact goods/service entry and the applicable notification for the time of supply. Do not assume that every 12% entry disappeared; verify the classification and current schedule before invoicing.

**Q: What happens to goods supplied around a rate-change date?** Section 14 has specific time-of-supply tests involving the supply, invoice and payment dates. The answer depends on which events occurred before or after the effective date; check the rule for the transaction rather than using a two-date shortcut.

**Q: Do I need a new GSTIN for GST 2.0?**  
A: No, your existing GSTIN remains perfectly valid. GST 2.0 is purely a rate and compliance system upgrade, not a re-registration drive.

## Summary

The 2025 GST rate rationalisation changed many entries, while later notifications addressed specified tobacco products from February 2026. It did not create a universal rate for a broad category or a July 2026 blanket reset. Keep product classifications, effective dates, pricing and ITC treatment tied to the relevant official notification.
