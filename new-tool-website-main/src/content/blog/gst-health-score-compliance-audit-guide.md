In modern tax governance, the Directorate General of Analytics and Risk Management (DGARM) and GST state enforcement wings rely heavily on artificial intelligence algorithms. A single systemic flaw in your internal accounting—such as claiming ITC without verifying whether your vendor has filed GSTR-3B, or exceeding the 20% variance threshold between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B—can automatically trigger pre-demand intimations in **Form GST DRC-01B** or **DRC-01C**, leading to the sudden suspension of your GSTIN.

In this guide, we introduce the **10-Point GST Health Score Framework** used by Chartered Accountants to audit enterprise compliance health and eliminate litigation exposure.

## The 10-Point GST Health Score Framework

Evaluating your business against these ten key parameters provides an immediate snapshot of your audit readiness:

### 1. Inward ITC Reconciliation (GSTR-3B vs GSTR-2B)
Under **Section 16(2)(aa) of the CGST Act**, a taxpayer cannot claim even a single rupee of Input Tax Credit unless the details of that invoice have been furnished by the supplier in their GSTR-1 / IFF and communicated to the buyer in **Form GSTR-2B**.  
- *High Risk:* Claiming ITC based merely on physical purchase invoices or ERP ledgers without 100% matching against GSTR-2B.

### 2. Form DRC-01C Discrepancy Margin
If the ITC claimed in Table 4(A)(5) of your GSTR-3B exceeds the eligible ITC appearing in GSTR-2B by more than **20% and ₹25 Lakhs**, the GST portal automatically generates a **Form DRC-01C intimation**.  
- You have strictly **7 days** to either pay the excess credit via DRC-03 or file an explanation; failure to do so blocks your next month's GSTR-1 filing.

### 3. Outward Supply Variance (GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B) & DRC-01B
Under **Rule 88C**, if the tax liability reported in GSTR-1 exceeds the tax paid in GSTR-3B by more than **20% and ₹25 Lakhs**, an automated **Form DRC-01B intimation** is triggered.  
- *Best Practice:* Ensure GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B are generated directly from the same validated sales register.

### 4. Vendor Risk & Non-Filer Verification
Under Section 16(2)(c), your right to ITC is contingent upon your supplier actually depositing the tax to the government.  
- If your vendors default on their GSTR-3B returns, tax officers will issue demand notices to **you** to reverse the credit along with 18% interest under Section 50. Regularly audit supplier filing statuses using our **[Bulk GSTIN Validator](/bulk-gstin-validator)**.

### 5. Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) Liability Discharge
Ensure that all inward liabilities subject to RCM (goods transport by GTA, legal services, directorship remuneration, and overseas software imports) are properly computed, deposited **in cash** in Table 3.1(d) of GSTR-3B, and only then claimed as ITC in Table 4(A)(3).

### 6. E-Invoicing & Rule 48(4) Compliance
If your aggregate turnover in any financial year from 2017-18 onwards exceeded **₹5 Crores**, every B2B and export invoice must bear a valid **Invoice Reference Number (IRN)** and signed QR code.  
- An invoice issued without an IRN is legally deemed invalid under Rule 48(5), disallowing ITC to your buyers.

### 7. Rule 42 / 43 Monthly Credit Reversals
If your business supplies both taxable and exempt goods/services, ensure you calculate and reverse proportionate common input credit monthly under **Rule 42** in Table 4(B)(1) of GSTR-3B, followed by an annual true-up in GSTR-9.

### 8. E-Way Bill vs Outward Turnovers
Verify that outward movement of goods exceeding ₹50,000 matches generated E-Way bills. DGARM flags any substantial mismatch where E-Way bill values diverge from reported GSTR-1 outward figures.

### 9. 180-Day Vendor Payment Rule (Section 16(2) Second Proviso)
Under the second proviso to Section 16(2), if you fail to pay the vendor the value of supply plus tax within **180 days from the invoice date**, you must reverse the claimed ITC along with 18% interest in your subsequent GSTR-3B. The credit can be reclaimed only once the vendor payment is cleared.

### 10. Filing Discipline & Due Date Adherence
Consistently filing GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, and GSTR-9 on or before the 11th, 20th, and 31st December without attracting late fees under Section 47 preserves a high compliance rating on the GST portal.

## Grading Your GST Health Score

| Score Range | Compliance Tier | Departmental Scrutiny Risk | Action Plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **90 – 100** | **Green Tier (Pristine)** | Extremely Low | Maintain automated 2B reconciliation; perform quarterly internal health checks. |

## What to Do If Your Score Is in the Red Tier

1. **Perform an Immediate Invoice-Level GSTR-2B Audit:** Stop claiming estimated ITC. Match purchase ledgers against GSTR-2B using specialized reconciliation utilities.
2. **Reconcile GSTR-1 with GSTR-3B:** Identify any sales reported in GSTR-1 that were missed in GSTR-3B, and deposit the shortfall voluntarily using Form DRC-03 to prevent Rule 88C blocking.
3. **Issue Vendor Compliance Notices:** Notify non-compliant suppliers that subsequent purchase payments will be withheld until their delinquent GSTR-3B returns are filed on the portal.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Can the GST portal block my E-Way bill generation?
Yes. Under **Rule 138E**, if a registered person fails to furnish GSTR-3B returns for **two consecutive tax periods**, the portal automatically blocks the generation of outward E-Way bills, effectively halting the movement of physical goods.

### How often should an enterprise audit its GST Health Score?
Growing enterprises and high-volume traders should conduct an internal health audit **monthly prior to filing GSTR-3B** (before the 20th), followed by a comprehensive quarterly management audit.
