Finding an order in **Form GST DRC-07** uploaded to your GST portal is a critical legal event. Unlike preliminary scrutiny notices (ASMT-10) or show cause notices (DRC-01), **DRC-07 is the formal Summary of the Order** passed by the Proper Officer creating a final, binding pecuniary debt against your business.

Once DRC-07 is uploaded, the clock starts ticking immediately: you have exactly **three months (90 days)** to either pay the adjudicated demand or file a statutory first appeal before the Commissioner (Appeals). If you do neither, the tax department is legally authorized under **Section 79** to initiate aggressive recovery proceedings, including freezing your bank accounts and seizing goods.

In this operational guide, we explain the legal ramifications of DRC-07, the precise 90-day defense roadmap, and how the **Section 128A Amnesty Scheme** can wipe out 100% of interest and penalties.

## What is Form GST DRC-07?

Under **Rule 142(5) of the CGST Rules, 2017**, whenever an adjudicating officer concludes assessment or adjudication proceedings under Sections 62, 63, 64, 73, 74, 122, 125, 127, 129, or 130, they must issue a speaking order along with a summary thereof in **Form GST DRC-07**.

### Key Contents of DRC-07:
1. **Reference Number & Date of Order**: Sets the legal limitation clock.
2. **Financial Year & Section**: Indicates whether demand was created under Section 73 (non-fraud) or Section 74 (fraud).
3. **Pecuniary Breakup**: Itemized liability across CGST, SGST, IGST, Cess, Interest under Section 50, and Penalties.
4. **Electronic Liability Ledger Posting**: The demand is automatically debited to your Liability Register Part II (Form GST PMT-01).

## The 90-Day Timeline: What Happens If You Do Nothing?

Under **Section 78 of the CGST Act**, any amount payable by a taxable person in pursuance of an order passed under this Act must be paid within a period of **three months (90 days)** from the date of service of the order.

```
Day 0: DRC-07 Issued & Debited to Electronic Liability Ledger
  │
  ├─► Days 1 to 90: Statutory Appeal Window (Form GST APL-01)
  │   Pay 10% Disputed Tax Pre-Deposit ➔ Automatic Stay on Recovery
  │
  └─► Day 91+: Section 79 Recovery Powers Triggered
      • Notice to Bankers (Sec 79(1)(c)) ➔ Bank Account Attachment
      • Notice to Debtors ➔ Garnishee Orders
      • Seizure & Auction of Movable/Immovable Assets
```

## The 90-Day Action Plan: 3 Strategic Pathways

### Pathway A: File a Statutory Appeal in Form GST APL-01 (Recommended If Disputed)
If you disagree with the officer's findings on factual or legal grounds:
1. File an appeal before the First Appellate Authority (Commissioner Appeals) under Section 107 within **3 months** (condonable by 1 month if sufficient cause is shown).
2. Pay the **mandatory 10% pre-deposit** of the disputed tax amount via electronic cash ledger.
3. The moment the appeal is acknowledged, the remaining **90% demand is automatically stayed**, barring the recovery officer from freezing your accounts.

### Pathway B: Apply for Section 128A Amnesty Scheme (Full Waiver of Interest & Penalties)
If your DRC-07 order:
- Was issued under **Section 73** (non-fraud), AND
- Pertains to **FY 2017-18, 2018-19, or 2019-20**:
You can avail full relief under Section 128A. By paying **100% of the basic tax amount** on or before the notified date, **100% of the interest and penalties are permanently waived**, and all proceedings are legally deemed concluded.

### Pathway C: File for Installment Facility under Section 80
If the demand is genuine but your business faces temporary liquidity constraints:
- Apply to the Commissioner under **Section 80** requesting permission to pay the demand in monthly installments (up to a maximum of **24 installments**).
- Note: The installment facility is not available for amounts less than ₹25,000 and carries statutory interest under Section 50.

## Common Grounds for Challenging DRC-07 Orders

1. **Ex-Parte Order without Personal Hearing:** If the Proper Officer issued DRC-07 without scheduling a personal hearing as mandated by **Section 75(4)**, file a Writ Petition before the High Court or challenge it in appeal for violation of natural justice.
2. **Order Beyond SCN Allegations:** Under **Section 75(7)**, the amount of tax, interest, and penalty demanded in the order cannot exceed the amount specified in the original DRC-01 notice, nor can the officer decide on grounds not raised in the SCN.
3. **Absence of a Reasoned (Speaking) Order:** A DRC-07 that merely states "Taxpayer reply examined and not satisfactory" without assigning detailed judicial reasons is bad in law.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### What is the difference between DRC-07 and DRC-08?
While DRC-07 is the original Summary of Demand passed by the adjudicating officer, **DRC-08 is the Summary of Rectification or Withdrawal Order** issued when an error apparent on the face of the record is corrected under Section 161.

### Can an officer attach my bank account before 90 days?
Under the proviso to Section 78, if the Proper Officer has compelling reasons to believe that the recovery of tax will be frustrated if 3 months are allowed, they may, with the prior approval of the Commissioner, require payment within an expedited period (e.g., 15 days). However, such power cannot be exercised routinely without written recording of emergency reasons.
