One of the most persistent myths among small business owners and traders in India is that **having a GST registration prevents you from opting for presumptive taxation under Section 44AD**.

This is completely false. Any eligible resident individual, HUF, or partnership firm (excluding LLPs) registered under GST can freely opt for the presumptive taxation scheme under Section 44AD of the Income Tax Act, 1961—provided their annual gross turnover remains within the statutory threshold.

In this guide, we analyze how GST turnover reconciles with Section 44AD, the revised ₹3 Crore turnover threshold, the 6% vs 8% deemed profit rates, and how to avoid costly tax audits under Section 44AB.

## Eligibility Criteria under Section 44AD

To adopt Section 44AD, the taxpayer must meet all the following conditions:

1. **Eligible Entity**: Must be an **Individual, Hindu Undivided Family (HUF), or Partnership Firm** resident in India. Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs), private limited companies, and public companies are strictly ineligible.
2. **Gross Turnover Limit**:
   - Standard limit: **₹2 Crore** per financial year.
   - Enhanced limit: **₹3 Crore**, provided aggregate cash receipts during the financial year do not exceed **5% of total turnover**.
3. **Excluded Businesses**:
   - Commission or brokerage agents.
   - Persons carrying on any agency business.
   - Professionals specified under Section 44AA(1) (who must use Section 44ADA instead).
   - Goods transport operators (covered under Section 44AE).

## 6% vs 8% Deemed Profit Computation

Under Section 44AD, you are not required to maintain complex books of accounts under Section 44AA or undergo a Chartered Accountant audit under Section 44AB. Your minimum net taxable business income is presumed to be:

- **6% of Gross Turnover** received through digital payment modes: account payee cheque, bank draft, ECS, RTGS, NEFT, IMPS, UPI, or debit/credit cards (received on or before the due date of filing ITR under Section 139(1)).
- **8% of Gross Turnover** received in physical cash or bearer instruments.

### Practical Example:
A wholesale electrical dealer has a total GST turnover of ₹1,80,00,000 for FY 2025-26:
- Digital collections (Bank/UPI): ₹1,50,00,000
- Cash collections: ₹30,00,000

**Minimum Deemed Profit:**
- Digital: ₹1,50,00,000 × 6% = **₹9,00,000**
- Cash: ₹30,00,000 × 8% = **₹2,40,000**  
**Total Deemed Business Income:** **₹11,40,000**

You can declare a profit higher than ₹11,40,000 if your actual earnings were higher, but declaring anything below this statutory threshold triggers mandatory maintenance of books and a tax audit.

## The 5-Year Lock-In Rule (Section 44AD(4))

Taxpayers must be cautious about switching in and out of the presumptive scheme:
- If you declare profits under Section 44AD in Year 1, you should ideally continue declaring profits under Section 44AD for the next **5 consecutive assessment years**.
- If you opt out of Section 44AD in any of these 5 years (by showing profits lower than 6%/8%), you are **barred from claiming Section 44AD for the subsequent 5 assessment years**.
- In the year you opt out, you must maintain complete double-entry books of account and undergo a mandatory tax audit under Section 44AB if your total income exceeds the basic exemption limit.

## Reconciling GST Returns (GSTR-1 & GSTR-3B) with ITR-4

One of the top triggers for an AI-generated Income Tax defect notice (DRC-01A or Section 139(9)) is a **discrepancy between the gross turnover reported in ITR-4 and the aggregate turnover reported in GSTR-1 / GSTR-3B**.

### How Income Tax AI Matches Data:
1. The Income Tax portal automatically fetches your annual outward taxable supplies from your GSTINs linked to your PAN.
2. If your ITR-4 shows turnover of ₹1.4 Crore, but your cumulative GSTR-1 outward supplies show ₹1.8 Crore, an automated mismatch notice is triggered under the e-Verification Scheme.

### Legitimate Reasons for Differences:
- **Exempt / Non-GST Supplies**: Alcohol, high-speed diesel, or petroleum products not subject to GST but part of business turnover.
- **Credit Notes and Discounts**: Ensure credit notes are accounted for consistently in both returns.
- **Advances Received**: GST is payable on advances for services, but for income tax, revenue is recognized on accrual.

## Advance Tax Rules for Section 44AD Taxpayers

Unlike normal business entities that must pay advance tax in four quarterly installments (15%, 45%, 75%, 100%), taxpayers opting for Section 44AD enjoy a major cash-flow relief:
- **100% of Advance Tax can be paid in a single installment** on or before **March 15th** of the financial year.
- No Section 234C interest penalty is levied for missing June, September, or December deadlines.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Can a GST Composition Scheme dealer use Section 44AD?
**Yes, absolutely.** The GST Composition Scheme (Section 10 of CGST Act) and Section 44AD of the Income Tax Act are independent laws. A small retail trader can pay 1% flat GST under the composition scheme and declare 6%/8% presumptive profits under Section 44AD in ITR-4.

### Are business expenses deductible from the 6% or 8% profit?
No. The presumptive rate of 6% or 8% is deemed to be net of all business expenses. All deductions allowable under Sections 30 to 38 (including depreciation, salary, electricity, and shop rent) are deemed to have already been given full effect. No separate expense deductions are permitted.

### Can partners' salary or interest on capital be deducted from 44AD income?
No. Post Finance Act amendment, partnership firms opting for Section 44AD cannot claim separate deductions for salary, remuneration, or interest paid to partners under Section 40(b).
