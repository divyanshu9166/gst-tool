Filing Form GST RFD-01 is only half the battle. For businesses with millions of rupees in working capital tied up in the government treasury, knowing exactly where the refund application sits in the departmental pipeline—and when the statutory clock runs out—is critical.

The Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act embeds strict statutory milestones to prevent bureaucratic delay. If the tax department fails to issue your refund within **60 days from the date of receipt of the application**, the government is legally mandated under **Section 56** to pay you **interest @ 6% per annum**.

In this guide, we map out the complete lifecycle of a GST refund application, decode every official form code, and provide actionable remedies if your refund gets stuck.

## The Complete GST Refund Processing Timeline

```
[Day 0: Application Filed (ARN Generated)]
                     │
                     ▼
[Day 1 to 15: Preliminary Scrutiny (Rule 90)]
  ├─► Complete? ➔ Form GST RFD-02 (Acknowledgement Issued)
  │                 (60-Day Clock Officially Starts)
  └─► Defective? ➔ Form GST RFD-03 (Deficiency Memo)
                    (Application Debited Credit Re-credited; Must File Fresh Claim)
                     │
                     ▼
[Day 1 to 22: Provisional Sanction for Exporters (Sec 54(6))]
  Form GST RFD-04 ➔ 90% of Claim Disbursed Provisionally
                     │
                     ▼
[Within 60 Days of RFD-02: Final Adjudication (Sec 54(7))]
  ├─► Accepted? ➔ Form GST RFD-06 (Sanction Order) + RFD-05 (Payment Order)
  │                 Funds Credited via PFMS / Bank Account
  └─► Rejected? ➔ Form GST RFD-08 (Show Cause Notice)
                    File Reply in Form RFD-09 within 15 Days
                     │
                     ▼
[Day 61+: Delay Penalties Triggered (Sec 56)]
  Mandatory Interest @ 6% p.a. Payable by Government to Taxpayer
```

## Decoding the Form Codes: What Each Status Means

| Form Code | Official Name | What It Means for the Taxpayer |
|---|---|---|
| **ARN** | Application Reference Number | Proof of successful filing on GST Portal. |
| **RFD-02** | Formal Acknowledgement | Proper Officer verifies documents are prima facie complete. **The 60-day clock begins here.** |
| **RFD-03** | Deficiency Memo | Notice that documents or reconciliations are missing. The original application is rejected and credit restored. |
| **RFD-04** | Provisional Refund Sanction Order | For zero-rated exporters: Proper Officer must sanction **90% of the claim** within 7 days of RFD-02. |
| **RFD-05** | Payment Order | Direct electronic instruction dispatched to PFMS / Reserve Bank of India to release funds to your bank account. |
| **RFD-06** | Final Refund Sanction / Rejection Order | Speaking order detailing approved refund amount and adjustments against any past confirmed arrears. |
| **RFD-08** | Notice for Rejection of Refund | SCN specifying reasons why part or whole of refund cannot be granted. |
| **RFD-09** | Reply to Notice | Formal response filed by taxpayer contesting RFD-08 within 15 days. |

## The Danger of RFD-03 (Deficiency Memo)

The issuance of **Form GST RFD-03** is the most frequent stalling tactic encountered by taxpayers.
- Under **Rule 90(3)**, a deficiency memo can only be issued within **15 days** of the date of filing.
- If a deficiency memo is issued, the original application is considered dead, and you must rectify the defect and file a **fresh application**—resetting the 60-day statutory clock.
- **CBIC Circular No. 125/44/2019-GST Safeguard:** The proper officer cannot issue repeated deficiency memos in a piecemeal manner. All deficiencies must be consolidated and conveyed in a single, initial RFD-03. A second deficiency memo on the same application is legally impermissible.

## 90% Provisional Refund for Exporters (Section 54(6))

Under Section 54(6) read with Rule 91, registered persons making zero-rated supplies of goods or services are entitled to receive **90% of the total claimed amount provisionally**:
- Must be sanctioned within **7 days** from the date of the acknowledgment (RFD-02).
- **Eligibility:** The applicant must not have been prosecuted for any tax evasion exceeding ₹2.5 Crores during any period of five years immediately preceding the tax period.
- The remaining 10% balance is verified and sanctioned under RFD-06 within the 60-day window.

## Claiming 6% Interest on Delayed Refunds (Section 56)

If your refund is sanctioned after 60 days from the date of receipt of the application, interest @ **6% per annum** is calculated from the day immediately after the expiry of 60 days until the date of actual payment.
- If the refund order in RFD-06 omits this interest, you can file an application for rectification under **Section 161**.
- In numerous rulings (such as *S.B. International* in Delhi High Court), courts have held that the payment of interest under Section 56 is mandatory and automatic, not discretionary.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Why is my refund status showing "PFMS Validation Pending"?
Before the central bank releases funds, your bank account must undergo validation by the Public Financial Management System (PFMS). If your bank account name does not match your GST registered trade/legal name, or if IFSC details are invalid, validation will fail. Update your primary bank account under Non-Core Field Amendment on the GST portal immediately.

### What should I do if my refund is rejected under RFD-06?
A rejection in RFD-06 is an appealable adjudicating order. You have the right to file a First Appeal under **Section 107** in Form GST APL-01 before the Commissioner (Appeals) within three months of the order.
