Selling on e-commerce platforms like Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho, or Myntra offers incredible reach for small businesses across India. However, the moment you transition from a local retail shop to an online seller, your Goods and Services Tax (GST) compliance requirements change drastically.

For supplies covered by Section 52, e-commerce operators report supply and TCS details in GSTR-8. Compare those reports with your marketplace settlement statements and GST returns, and investigate differences before filing.

This guide explains the main registration, TCS, return, and sales-return checks for e-commerce sellers. Verify the rules that apply to your products, State, and tax period.

## 1. Mandatory GST Registration (The New Exceptions)

Historically, anyone selling goods through an e-commerce platform was required to obtain a GST registration from day one, regardless of their turnover. 

However, recent amendments have provided a massive relief for micro-sellers:
- **Intra-State Goods Exception:** Notification 34/2023-Central Tax waives registration for certain goods suppliers using e-commerce operators when their aggregate turnover stays below the applicable registration threshold in the preceding and current financial years. The supplier must meet the notification conditions, including no inter-State goods supplies, a single State or Union Territory for these platform supplies, PAN declaration, and portal enrolment before supplying.
- **Inter-State Goods Sales:** The Notification 34/2023 exemption does not cover inter-State goods supplies. Check registration and any other applicable statutory exception before accepting such orders.

*Note:* This notification concerns goods supplied through eligible e-commerce operators. Service suppliers follow different provisions. Read [Notification 34/2023-Central Tax](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/node/4389) and the current portal enrolment instructions before relying on the exception.

## 2. Understanding TCS (Tax Collected at Source)

When you make a covered sale through an e-commerce operator, it collects GST TCS under Section 52 on the *net value of taxable supplies*. [Notification 15/2024-Central Tax](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/node/5015) sets the current total rate at **0.5%**: 0.25% CGST plus 0.25% SGST/UTGST for intra-state supplies, or 0.5% IGST for inter-state supplies.

**The Math Example:**
- You sell a pair of shoes for ₹1,000 (Base Price) + ₹180 (18% GST). Total = ₹1,180.
- The customer returns another pair of shoes worth ₹200 (Base Price).
- Net Taxable Sales for the month = ₹800.
- At the current 0.5% rate, estimated TCS on ₹800 is **₹4**.

Use our [TCS under GST Calculator](/tcs-gst-calculator) to estimate the deduction. The marketplace statement and applicable adjustments determine the final amount.

## 3. The GSTR-8 Reconciliation Trap

E-commerce operators report the TCS they deducted against your GSTIN by filing their monthly **GSTR-8** return. 

The data they file in GSTR-8 directly reflects in your **TDS/TCS Credit Received** dashboard on the GST portal. You must manually "Accept" this data to transfer the TCS amount into your Electronic Cash Ledger, which you can then use to pay your monthly GST liability.

**When the numbers differ:**
A mismatch between an operator statement, your books, GSTR-1, and GSTR-3B calls for review. Returns, credit notes, timing, and the supplies covered by Section 52 can affect the comparison. Do not assume a fixed greater-than rule or an automatic ASMT-10 notice. Reconcile the underlying transactions and correct any filing error under the applicable rules.

## 4. Handling Sales Returns Correctly

E-commerce businesses face high return rates (RTOs and Customer Returns). How you handle these returns in GST is critical:
- Record each customer return against the original supply and the marketplace statement.
- Issue and report a GST credit note where required by the applicable rules, and check the relevant filing period and time limit. Do not simply reduce an unrelated month's sales to absorb an earlier return.

## 5. Composition Scheme Restrictions

Composition taxpayers may make certain intra-state goods supplies through e-commerce operators under the special procedure in [Notification 36/2023-Central Tax](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/node/4391). Separately, eligible unregistered goods suppliers may use the enrolment route under [Notification 34/2023-Central Tax](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/node/4389). Check the conditions for your own category and the marketplace's onboarding rules before selling.

## Conclusion

Download your marketplace settlement and tax reports regularly. Compare taxable supplies, returns, fees, and TCS with your records and the GST portal. Resolve unexplained differences before filing; the correct treatment depends on the transaction and period.
