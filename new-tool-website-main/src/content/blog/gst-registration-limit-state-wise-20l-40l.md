Under the Indian Goods and Services Tax (GST) system, not every small business or neighborhood trader is required to register for GST. To alleviate the compliance burden on micro-enterprises and informal traders, **Section 22 of the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017** provides turnover-based threshold exemptions.

However, the threshold is not uniform across India. Depending on whether you deal exclusively in goods, provide services, or operate from a Normal State vs. a **Special Category State**, your mandatory threshold can be **₹10 Lakhs**, **₹20 Lakhs**, or **₹40 Lakhs**.

In this guide, we break down the state-wise GST registration matrix, the definition of aggregate turnover, and mandatory registration scenarios under Section 24 where threshold limits do not apply.

## Conditions for Claiming the Enhanced ₹40 Lakh Limit for Goods

The ₹40 Lakh threshold introduced under **Notification No. 10/2019-Central Tax** is subject to strict statutory provisos. A business qualifies for ₹40 Lakhs only if:

1. **Exclusive Supply of Goods:** The taxpayer must deal **exclusively in goods**. If a trader also provides taxable services (e.g., equipment repair or installation charges), the threshold drops immediately to **₹20 Lakhs**.  
   *(Exception: Earning exempt interest on bank deposits, FDs, or loans does not disqualify a trader from the ₹40L goods threshold).*
2. **Intra-State Supplies Only:** The supplier must make supplies **only within the same state**. The moment a business makes even a single inter-state supply, Section 24 kicks in, requiring registration from Rupee 1.
3. **Not Engaged in Specified Commodities:** Manufacturers of ice cream, pan masala, tobacco, brick kilns, and earthen tiles are barred from the ₹40L limit and must register at ₹20L.
4. **Not Selling via E-Commerce Platforms:** Selling through Amazon, Flipkart, or Meesho removes threshold protection.

## What Counts in "Aggregate Turnover"? (Section 2(6))

When determining whether your business has crossed ₹10L, ₹20L, or ₹40L, the calculation is based on **Aggregate Turnover computed on an all-India PAN basis**:

$$\text{Aggregate Turnover} = \text{Taxable Supplies} + \text{Exempt Supplies} + \text{Zero-Rated Export Supplies} + \text{Inter-State Supplies}$$

### Critical Rules:
- **Exempt Sales Count:** If you sell ₹35 Lakhs worth of exempt fresh foodgrains and ₹6 Lakhs worth of taxable branded oils, your aggregate turnover is ₹41 Lakhs. You cross the ₹40L threshold and must obtain GST registration.
- **All Branches Combined:** If you operate three branch offices in different states under the same PAN, the turnover of all three locations is aggregated together.
- **What is Excluded?** Taxes paid under CGST, SGST, IGST, and inward supplies taxable under Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) are excluded.

## Section 24: Compulsory Registration Regardless of Turnover

Under **Section 24 of the CGST Act**, threshold limits are completely overridden in twelve specific cases. You must obtain GST registration **even if your annual sales are only ₹10,000**:

1. **Inter-State Taxable Supply of Goods:** Shipping goods across state borders (except for designated handicraft goods exemptions).
2. **Casual Taxable Persons (CTP):** Individuals setting up temporary exhibition stalls or pop-up stores in another state.
3. **Persons Liable to Pay Tax under Reverse Charge (RCM):** Businesses receiving goods transport (GTA) services or legal services liable to RCM.
4. **Non-Resident Taxable Persons (NRTP):** Foreign entities conducting commercial transactions in India.
5. **E-Commerce Sellers:** Suppliers selling goods through e-commerce operators like Amazon and Flipkart (subject to recent small-seller composition relaxations).
6. **Input Service Distributors (ISD):** Head offices distributing common service credits across multi-state branches.
7. **E-Commerce Operators:** Platforms liable to collect TCS under Section 52 or pay tax under Section 9(5).

## Voluntary GST Registration (Section 25(3))

Can you register for GST even if your turnover is below the threshold?  
**Yes.** Under Section 25(3), any business can apply for **Voluntary Registration**:
- **Why register voluntarily?** To claim Input Tax Credit on capital goods and commercial premises rent, supply to B2B corporate clients who require a GST invoice, or sell goods through inter-state channels.
- **Obligation:** Once registered voluntarily, you are subject to all standard GST compliance rules, including mandatory monthly/quarterly return filings and late fees.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### What is the penalty for failing to register for GST when turnover crosses the threshold?
Under **Section 122(1)(xi) of the CGST Act**, failing to register when liable attracts a penalty of **100% of the tax due or ₹10,000**, whichever is higher. Additionally, all sales made during the unregistered period become liable to tax with interest under Section 50.

### How much time do I have to apply for registration once I cross the threshold?
Under Section 25(1), you must submit your GST registration application in Form GST REG-01 within **30 days** from the date on which your turnover crossed the statutory threshold limit.
