Fitness clubs, crossfit boxes, pilates studios, and commercial gyms are among the fastest growing retail wellness businesses in urban India. However, gym operators often struggle with indirect tax compliance, personal training commission splits, and whether yoga training qualifies for special exemptions.

Under the Goods and Services Tax (GST) framework, commercial gym memberships are classified as physical fitness services subject to the **standard 18% GST rate**.

In this guide, we break down applicable SAC codes, the taxation of personal training and nutritional supplements, charitable yoga trust exemptions, and how gym owners can claim substantial Input Tax Credit (ITC) on commercial gym equipment.

## The SAC Code Classification & 18% GST Rate

Under the GST tariff schedule, gym and fitness center memberships fall under **Services Accounting Code (SAC) 999723** (*Physical fitness and wellness services*):

- **GST Rate:** **18%** (9% CGST + 9% SGST for intra-state memberships, or 18% IGST).
- **Turnover Threshold:** Gym owners must register for GST once their aggregate turnover (including membership fees, registration charges, locker rents, and merchandise sales) exceeds **₹20 Lakhs** in a financial year (₹10 Lakhs in Special Category States).

## The Yoga Exemption Myth: Commercial vs Charitable Trusts

A common misconception in the wellness industry is that "Yoga is exempt from GST in India".  
Under **Entry 1 of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate)**:
> *"Services by an entity registered under Section 12AA / 12AB of the Income Tax Act by way of charitable activities are EXEMPT from GST."*

Under CBDT and CBIC definitions, "advancement of yoga" is recognized as a charitable activity **only if provided by an entity holding a valid Section 12AA/12AB registration** from the Income Tax department (such as recognized spiritual foundations or non-profit ashrams).  
- If a commercial gym, private pilates studio, or independent fitness instructor charges ₹3,000/month for yoga classes, **18% GST is fully applicable**.

## Claiming Input Tax Credit (ITC) on Gym Equipment

One of the largest financial advantages of obtaining GST registration for a gym is the ability to claim **100% Input Tax Credit on capital equipment purchases**:

- **Cardio Equipment:** Treadmills, elliptical trainers, spin bikes (taxed at 18% or 28% under HSN 9506).
- **Strength Equipment:** Multi-gym machines, cable crossovers, dumbbells, barbells, rubber flooring (taxed at 18% or 28%).
- **Interior & Facilities:** Air conditioners, sound systems, biometric access turnstiles, CCTV cameras (taxed at 18% or 28%).
- **Premises Rent:** 18% GST charged by the commercial building landlord on monthly lease rent.

### Real-World Working Capital Example:
Setting up a modern commercial gym typically involves ₹30 Lakhs to ₹50 Lakhs in equipment and interior fit-outs, carrying roughly **₹5 Lakhs to ₹9 Lakhs in GST**.  
- By registering for GST prior to placing equipment orders, the gym operator receives this entire amount as **Input Tax Credit (ITC)** in their electronic credit ledger.
- This credit can then be used to offset output GST collected on membership fees for several subsequent months, protecting cash flows during the critical initial operational phase.

## Structuring Personal Training (PT) Commissions

In most fitness clubs, certified freelance personal trainers operate on a revenue-share model (e.g., 60% to trainer, 40% to gym):
- **Billing to Client:** The gym issues an invoice to the client for the full PT package (e.g., ₹10,000 + 18% GST = ₹11,800).
- **Payout to Trainer:** If the personal trainer is not GST registered (earning below ₹20L), the gym pays them their contractual share. The gym cannot deduct GST from the trainer unless the trainer issues a registered GST invoice. However, the gym must withhold TDS under **Section 194J(1)(a)** (Fees for professional/technical services) or Section 194H where applicable.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Can a gym opt for the GST Composition Scheme?
Under Section 10(2A) of the CGST Act, service providers with an aggregate annual turnover of up to **₹50 Lakhs** can opt for the Presumptive GST Scheme, paying a flat **6% GST (3% CGST + 3% SGST)** without Input Tax Credit. However, if a gym opts for this, it cannot claim ITC on its expensive initial equipment purchases.

### Is GST applicable on unutilized membership cancellations or no-show fees?
Yes. Under Schedule II, agreeing to the obligation to refrain from an act or tolerating an act is deemed a supply of services. Forfeited membership fees or non-refundable cancellation charges remain subject to **18% GST**.
