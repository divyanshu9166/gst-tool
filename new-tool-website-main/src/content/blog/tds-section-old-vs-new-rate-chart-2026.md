Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is the cornerstone of India's direct tax compliance system. For accounts teams, chartered accountants, and business owners, deducting the exact statutory percentage on commercial payments is critical to avoid harsh disallowances under **Section 40(a)(ia)** and mandatory 1%–1.5% per month interest under **Section 201(1A)**.

Recent union budgets and CBDT notifications have adjusted key thresholds and rationalized multiple TDS provisions—including the reduction of rates for e-commerce operators, professional fees, and mutual fund repurchases.

## The Danger of Non-PAN: Section 206AA & 206AB

### Section 206AA: Higher Rates for Missing PAN
If the vendor or payee fails to furnish a valid PAN, the tax deductor is statutorily mandated to deduct TDS at the **highest** of:
1. The rate specified in the relevant provision of the Act.
2. The rates in force.
3. **20%** (or 5% in specific sections like 194Q/194O).

### Section 206AB: Higher Rates for "Specified Persons" (Non-Filers)
Under Section 206AB, if a vendor has not filed their Income Tax Return for the previous assessment year (where the time limit under Sec 139(1) has expired) and their aggregate TDS/TCS exceeded **₹50,000** in that year, TDS must be deducted at **twice the normal rate or 5%**, whichever is higher. Always run a check on the Income Tax Portal's "Compliance Check for Section 206AB & 206CCA" prior to large disbursements.

## TDS Deposit Due Dates and Quarterly Return Deadlines

TDS deducted during any calendar month must be deposited to the central government via Challan ITNS 281 by the **7th of the following month** (except for March deductions, which can be deposited up to April 30th).

| Quarter | Period Covered | Form 24Q / 26Q / 27Q Filing Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **Q1** | April 1 to June 30 | July 31 |
| **Q2** | July 1 to September 30 | October 31 |
| **Q3** | October 1 to December 31 | January 31 |
| **Q4** | January 1 to March 31 | May 31 |

> **Late Filing Fee under Section 234E:** Delay in filing TDS returns incurs a mandatory late fee of **₹200 per day** of default until the return is furnished, capped at the total TDS amount.

## Common Practical Dilemmas

### 1. Section 194C vs 194J: Contractor vs Technical Service
If a vendor provides customized software development, is it contract work (194C @ 2%) or technical services (194J @ 2% or 10%)?  
Courts have ruled that where intellectual property, human expertise, and code creation are predominant, it qualifies under Section 194J(1)(b) as Technical or Professional services. However, pure maintenance contracts (AMCs) without engineering redesign often qualify under Section 194C.

### 2. Section 194Q vs TCS Section 206C(1H)
Both sections deal with high-value goods transactions exceeding ₹50 Lakhs.  
- **Rule of Priority:** Section 194Q (buyer's obligation to deduct TDS) takes precedence over Section 206C(1H) (seller's obligation to collect TCS). If the buyer has turnover above ₹10 Crores in the preceding FY, the buyer must deduct TDS under 194Q, and the seller must NOT collect TCS.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Can I claim refund of excess TDS deducted?
Yes. When you file your annual ITR, all TDS credits shown in **Form 26AS / AIS (Annual Information Statement)** are adjusted against your net income tax liability. Any excess TDS is automatically refunded directly to your pre-validated bank account with 0.5% monthly interest under Section 244A.

### Is TDS applicable on GST component of the invoice?
No. Under CBDT Circular No. 23/2017, if the GST component (CGST, SGST, IGST) is indicated separately on the tax invoice, TDS must be deducted **only on the basic value of goods/services**, excluding the GST amount.

### What is the penalty for failing to deduct TDS?
Failure to deduct TDS attracts equal penalty under Section 271C, 1% per month interest under Section 201(1A), and **30% disallowance of the entire expenditure** under Section 40(a)(ia) while computing your business profits.
