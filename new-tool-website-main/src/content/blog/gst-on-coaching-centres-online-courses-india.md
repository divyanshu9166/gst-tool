India's test preparation and EdTech ecosystem is one of the largest in the world. However, there is pervasive confusion among students, educators, and academy founders regarding indirect taxation: **Is education exempt from GST in India?**

The short legal answer is: **No, private coaching institutes and online course creators are NOT exempt.**

While formal degree-granting educational institutions (schools, colleges, and recognized universities) enjoy broad statutory exemptions under Entry 66 of Notification 12/2017, commercial coaching centers, UPSC/JEE academies, skill development workshops, and online EdTech platforms are subject to a **mandatory 18% GST**.

In this guide, we break down what separates exempt formal education from taxable coaching, the taxation of study materials and test series, and how to handle international course sales.

## The Core Education Exemption: Entry 66 of Notification 12/2017

Under **Entry 66 of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate)**, services provided by an **educational institution** to its students, faculty, and staff are exempt from GST.

### The Strict Statutory Definition of "Educational Institution":
For the purpose of GST, an educational institution is strictly defined as an entity providing:
1. Pre-school education and education up to higher secondary school (Class 12) or equivalent.
2. Education as a part of a curriculum for obtaining a **qualification recognized by any Indian law for the time being in force** (e.g., UGC-approved degrees, AICTE diplomas, ICAI, ICSI, NMC medical degrees).
3. Education as a part of an approved vocational education course (NCVT / SCVT).

### Why Coaching Centers & Online Courses Do NOT Qualify:
Private coaching classes (Kota institutes, UPSC academies), EdTech platforms (Coursera, Udemy, Unacademy, PhysicsWallah), and independent course creators do **not** award degrees recognized by an act of Parliament or state legislature. Consequently, **they do not qualify as "educational institutions" under GST law**.

## Bundling Coaching with Printed Books & Tablets: Composite vs Mixed Supply

Many competitive exam institutes bundle physical study materials, printed test modules, or Android tablets with their coaching subscriptions. How should this be billed?

### 1. Composite Supply (Section 2(30))
If the printed books and mock tests are naturally bundled and integral to the coaching course, and the coaching is the principal supply:
- The entire bundled fee is taxed at the rate of the principal supply: **18% GST on the full package price**.
- Even though printed books have a 0% GST rate on a standalone basis, you cannot artificially bifurcate the fee to claim exemption when bundled in an all-inclusive tuition package.

### 2. Separate Standalone Sale (Unbundled)
If the coaching institute runs a distinct publishing wing and students have the genuine option to enroll in classes without purchasing books:
- **Tuition Fee:** Billed under SAC 999293 @ **18% GST**.
- **Printed Books & Study Notes:** Billed under HSN 4901 @ **0% GST**.
- This unbundled billing structure is completely legal and can lower the overall tax burden for students, provided books are genuinely optional and sold at arm's length commercial prices.

## Selling Online Courses to Overseas Students (Exports)

If you create digital courses or run a cohort program and sell subscriptions to overseas students (e.g., in the US, Dubai, or Singapore):
- The service qualifies as an **Export of Services under Section 2(6)** of the IGST Act.
- By filing an annual **Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** in Form GST RFD-11, you can bill overseas students at **0% GST**.
- Payment must be received in convertible foreign exchange (or approved Vostro INR) via Stripe, PayPal, or international wire.

## Input Tax Credit for Coaching Centers

Because private coaching academies collect 18% GST on course fees, they are entitled to claim **full Input Tax Credit (ITC)** on operational costs:
- Commercial rent paid on classrooms and study halls (18% GST).
- Studio recording cameras, microphones, and interactive digital smart-boards (18% GST).
- SaaS subscriptions: Zoom licenses, LMS platforms, website hosting, and video CDNs (18% GST).
- Marketing and advertising agency retainers (18% GST).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### What is the GST threshold limit for an individual online tutor?
Under Section 22, an individual tutor or creator providing online or offline classes within their home state is only required to register for GST once their aggregate annual fee collection exceeds **₹20 Lakhs** (₹10 Lakhs in Special Category States).

### Are placement agency services provided by institutes taxable?
Yes. Providing campus placement assistance or charging recruiting companies placement fees is classified as an auxiliary commercial service and is subject to **18% GST** under SAC 998514.
