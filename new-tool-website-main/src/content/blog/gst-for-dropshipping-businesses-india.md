Dropshipping has emerged as one of the most lucrative digital business models in India. By marketing products via Shopify, WooCommerce, or Meta ads without holding physical inventory, Indian entrepreneurs generate significant revenues.

However, dropshipping taxation is intensely scrutinized by the Indian tax department. Depending on whether your supplier, your business, and your end-customer are located inside or outside India, your GST liability can range from **18% domestic GST**, **18% export tax**, or **complete tax immunity under Schedule III (Out-and-Out Supplies)**.

In this guide, we analyze the two fundamental dropshipping models and how to structure your business legally under Indian tax laws.

---

## Model 1: Domestic Dropshipping (India to India)

In a domestic dropshipping model:
- **Merchant (You):** Located in India (e.g., Delhi).
- **Supplier:** Located in India (e.g., Surat).
- **Customer:** Located in India (e.g., Bengaluru).

```
[Customer Pays ₹2,000] ──► [Indian Dropshipper] ──► [Surat Supplier Paid ₹800]
                                   │                              │
                                   └────── Invoices Customer ◄────┘
                                           Ships Directly
```

### GST Obligations for Domestic Dropshipping:
1. **Mandatory GST Registration:** Under **Section 24(ix) of the CGST Act**, any person who supplies goods through an e-commerce platform or engages in inter-state supplies of goods is **mandatorily required to register for GST**, irrespective of whether their turnover is below ₹40 Lakhs.
2. **Back-to-Back Tax Invoicing:**
   - **Leg 1 (Supplier to Dropshipper):** The Surat supplier bills you ₹800 + 18% GST (₹144) = ₹944. (You claim ₹144 as Input Tax Credit).
   - **Leg 2 (Dropshipper to Customer):** You bill the retail customer ₹2,000 including 18% GST (Basic ₹1,694.92 + GST ₹305.08).
   - **Tax Liability in GSTR-3B:** Your output tax is ₹305.08. You offset your ₹144 ITC, paying the net ₹161.08 in cash.
3. **Bill To - Ship To Model:** In your invoice and E-Way bill, the supplier's address is listed as "Dispatch From", your company as "Supplier / Bill To", and the end customer as "Ship To" under **Section 10(1)(b)**.

## Model 2: International / Cross-Border Dropshipping (e.g., China to USA)

In international dropshipping:
- **Merchant (You):** Located in India.
- **Supplier:** Located in China, AliExpress, CJ Dropshipping, or a US 3PL warehouse.
- **Customer:** Located in the United States, UK, Europe, or Australia.
- **The Physical Goods:** Never touch Indian territorial waters or customs ports.

```
[US Customer Pays $100] ──► [Indian Merchant] ──► [AliExpress / CJ Dropshipper $35]
                                  │                             │
                                  └────── Ships Directly from China to US
```

### Is GST Applicable on Foreign-to-Foreign Goods?
**No. These transactions are strictly non-taxable under Schedule III.**

Under **Paragraph 7 of Schedule III to the CGST Act** (inserted by the CGST Amendment Act, 2018):
> *"Supply of goods from a place in the non-taxable territory to another place in the non-taxable territory without such goods entering into India shall be treated neither as a supply of goods nor a supply of services."*

These transactions are legally classified as **"Out-and-Out Supplies"** or **"Merchant Trade Transactions (MTT)"**:
- **GST Rate:** **0% (Non-taxable supply under Schedule III)**.
- **GSTR-1 Reporting:** Must be reported in **Table 8 of GSTR-1** under *Non-GST / Non-taxable outward supplies*.
- **No LUT Required for Goods:** Because the goods are outside the scope of GST under Schedule III, you do not need to file an LUT under Rule 96A for the merchandise.

## The Big Catch: Are You an "Intermediary"?

While the supply of physical goods from China to the USA is outside GST under Schedule III, tax officers frequently scrutinize cross-border dropshippers alleging they are **"Intermediaries" under Section 2(13) of the IGST Act**.

### How to Protect Your Dropshipping Business:
- If the department classifies you as an "agent or broker arranging supplies between foreign buyer and foreign seller", your place of supply defaults to India under **Section 13(8)(b)**, attracting a crushing **18% IGST liability** on your gross revenues.
- **To Prove Principal-to-Principal Status:**
  1. Your store terms of service must state that your company is the **Merchant of Record (seller)**, not an affiliate or booking agent.
  2. You must assume inventory and commercial risk (e.g., handling customer returns, chargebacks, and product warranties).
  3. The payment gateway (Stripe, PayPal, Shopify Payments) must settle funds to you directly as product sales revenue.

---

## Input Tax Credit Reversal under Rule 42

Because out-and-out dropshipping transactions under Paragraph 7 of Schedule III are non-taxable supplies:
- Under Section 17(2) read with Rule 42, Input Tax Credit on common business expenses (such as office electricity, local marketing retainers, or accounting software) must be **proportionately reversed** based on the ratio of exempt dropshipping turnover to total turnover.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Do I need GST registration if I only do international dropshipping?
Even though overseas dropshipping is a Schedule III non-taxable supply, banks and payment processors (such as Stripe India, PayPal, Razorpay) strictly require a valid GSTIN to enable business foreign currency payment gateways and settle export earnings to Indian current accounts.

### Can an Indian dropshipper accept payments via Stripe India?
Yes. When using Stripe India for cross-border e-commerce, Stripe processes international card payments and remits funds to your Indian bank account via an Authorized Dealer (AD) bank along with Foreign Inward Remittance Advices (FIRA). Ensure you file FIRA proofs during annual income tax assessments.
